Shop & product pages

Where a saree has to be sold on a photograph. The gallery does most of the work.

The gallery

  • Thumbnail rail, hover-to-zoom and a full-screen lightbox with keyboard and swipe support.

Offers & promotions

All of it without a promo code. Nothing for a shopper to type, and nothing for them to miss.

%
Buy 2 Get 1 Free Add any 3 sarees — the cheapest is on us

Pick the products an offer covers, by hand or by whole category, with an exclusion list. When enough are in the cart, the cheapest ones come off the total on their own.

Three sarees at ₹3,999, ₹2,999 and ₹1,200 → the ₹1,200 one is free. Six sarees → two free. Buy 3 get 1 works the same way, and so does 50% off the cheapest instead of free.

Quantity breaks

The other shape of offer — 2 for 10% off, 3 for 15%, 5 for 20%. Counted per product, so it catches the shopper buying a pair of the same saree, which the offer above deliberately does not. The breaks show as chips on the product page, and a shopper always gets the best one their quantity earns.

Complete the look

On any product, pick the pieces that go with it — the jhumkas for a saree, the bangles, a matching blouse. Under the product they read as this piece + match + match, each with a tick box, a running total, and one button that puts the whole look in the bag.

  • Unticking a piece re-totals instantly; a piece already in the bag says so rather than going in twice.
  • Give the pairing a percentage and it becomes a real offer — the matching pieces are discounted whenever the product they were chosen for is in the same cart.
  • Tick show this both ways and the saree turns up beside the jewellery too, without picking it again from that side.

Urgency, and a nudge

Countdown

Give an offer an end time and a live countdown appears on every product it covers and at the top of the cart.

Free-shipping meter

Add ₹340 more for free shipping, with a bar that fills and turns green. In the bag panel, the cart and above checkout.

How the offers hold together

The part that matters when real money is involved.

They never stack by accident

An offer and a pairing never both cut the same line, and no line can go below zero. The first to claim a line keeps it.

Worked out on the server

Not in the browser. It holds with JavaScript off, survives a refresh, and cannot be applied twice by a recalculation.

Written onto the order

The offer's name is recorded on the order line, so months later it is clear why a saree went out at nothing.

Visible, not hidden

The free line is struck through and marked in the cart, so the shopper knows what they are getting before they pay.

Running as many as you like

Offers are separate records, so sarees can have one and jewellery another — counted separately, so two sarees and two bangles is not three of anything. Add a third for a festival week and switch it off afterwards.

Cart, checkout & account

The last few screens, where most shops lose the sale.

  • A cart built for this theme — not WooCommerce's default blocks — with the free-shipping meter, a savings line, and a clear mark on every discounted line.
  • A three-step checkout with styled fields and a progress indicator, so a customer always knows how far along they are.
  • A slide-out bag from any page, with the free-shipping meter inside it.
  • Sign in and sign up on one designed page, not WordPress's grey login form.
  • My Account — orders, addresses, downloads and details, all in the theme's look, with delivery tracking built into each order.
  • A switch on the welcome screen to move between the theme's cart and WooCommerce's block version, whenever you want.

Wishlist and compare

Both included and both styled — a wishlist a shopper can build across visits, and a side-by-side compare table for choosing between two sarees on fabric, work, price and rating.

Track Your Order

A page of its own, where a customer enters an order number and the email or phone they used, and sees exactly where the parcel is — without needing an account.

What the customer sees

That progress line on the thank-you page, in My Account, and under the Track Your Order form — with the tracking number, the courier, the expected date, and every scan with its location and time. They can track without logging in: order number plus the email or phone from the order.

What the shop sees

A Delivery panel on every order — status, consignment number, courier, expected date and the full history. A Delivery column on the orders list, and bulk actions to mark a batch dispatched or delivered.

Three ways the status gets in

1. Delhivery answers
for itself		 Paste a Delhivery API token, pick an interval, and the shop asks them where every parcel still in flight is — moving orders along with nobody touching it. A Check now button too.
2. A push from
your delivery app		 A secure address your app can post each scan to, so the website says Out for delivery the moment the app does.
3. The courier's
CSV manifest		 Upload the two-column file Delhivery hands back after a pickup and every tracking number lands at once.

Told without being asked

Optional emails to the customer on dispatch — with the tracking link — and on delivery, in the shop's own styling. Optionally mark the WooCommerce order Completed the moment the parcel lands.

Delhivery's own wording is mapped onto the six stages above. Anything unfamiliar is recorded as a note rather than guessed at, so the customer is never told something that is not true.

The legal pages are written, not blank

All four arrive with a full draft written for an Indian e-commerce business — GST, cash on delivery, the Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023, the Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules 2020, and a named grievance officer as those rules require.

Typed once, used everywhere.

Your business name, GSTIN, address, email, phone, return window, shipping rates and cash-on-delivery limit are entered once in the Customizer and flow into every document — and into the product data Google reads.

Each policy page gets a contents rail down the side and a highlights strip at the top, so a customer can find the answer to one question without reading the whole document.

These are a well-researched starting point written for a business of your kind — not a substitute for your own legal advice. Read them through and adjust anything that does not match how you actually trade.

Our Story, Contact and FAQs

An editorial About page with the numbers, the weaving clusters you buy from, what you stand for and how a piece reaches the customer. A Contact page with the address, phone, email and hours. An FAQ page ready to fill in.

Found on Google

The parts of search that a shop can actually control, done properly.

  • Titles and descriptions with sensible defaults on every page, product and category.
  • Open Graph and Twitter cards, so a link shared on WhatsApp or Instagram shows the product photo rather than a blank box.
  • Structured data for products — price, availability, rating, brand, GTIN — plus breadcrumbs, the organisation and the site search. This is the mark-up Google uses to show a price and stars directly in the results.
  • Cart, checkout, account and search kept out of Google's index and out of the sitemap, where they only ever waste crawl budget.
  • Defers to your SEO plugin. Install Yoast, Rank Math or SEO Framework and the theme steps back, so nothing is ever printed twice.
